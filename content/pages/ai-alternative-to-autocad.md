Short answer: not yet. No AI tool today can take over the daily work of an AutoCAD user from start to finish. But several pieces of that work are already automated, and they are improving quickly.

## What replacing AutoCAD really means

Most AutoCAD work is not modelling. It is producing and maintaining 2D drawings that a workshop, a contractor or a customer can build from: views, dimensions, tolerances, title blocks, revisions, all in DWG. A real replacement has to do four things well:

- **Produce production drawings** that follow your company standard, not just pictures.
- **Work on existing drawings and models**, because most jobs start from something that already exists.
- **Create new geometry from a description or a sketch** with exact dimensions.
- **Give the same correct result every time**, so an engineer can trust it without redrawing.

## Where AI stands on each

**Drawings** are the most advanced area. Tools such as DraftAid and Autodesk's own drawing automation in Fusion generate views, dimensions and annotations from a 3D model. The catch: they need a 3D model first, which most AutoCAD-only workflows don't have.

**Existing work** is partly covered. Drawing tools work on your existing 3D models, and scan-to-CAD tools such as Backflip rebuild old parts from scans. Editing an existing 2D DWG by instruction is still limited to simple commands.

**Creating new parts** from text works for simple, well-dimensioned parts in tools like Zoo and Adam, but the output is 3D, not a finished 2D drawing.

**Consistency** is what nobody has proven yet. That is why we run our own benchmark with exact-dimension parts.

## What Autodesk is doing inside AutoCAD

AutoCAD's own AI, Autodesk Assistant, answers questions, checks drawings against CAD standards and reads the drawing's context. Markup Assist can carry out simple commands such as move, copy and delete from marked-up PDFs. Useful, but the drafting itself is still done by you.

## What to use today

If you draft mechanical parts in AutoCAD, the fastest gain is not an AI replacement but a workflow change: model the part in 3D, then let an AI drawing tool produce the 2D sheets. Keep AutoCAD for layouts, schematics and edits to legacy DWGs. Watch the tracker below: the first tool that scores well on drawings, existing work and consistency at the same time is the one that will start replacing AutoCAD seats.
